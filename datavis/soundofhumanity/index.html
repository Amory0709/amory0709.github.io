about the sound of life

A moment in the life of a country, told through the rhythm of births and deaths. This recreation preserves historical settings; it does not describe today’s U.S. population.

Each yellow dot marks a simulated birth; each red dot marks a simulated death. A pulse appears at its location, then the dot falls and joins the lives accumulating below the map.

At 100% speed, the simulation creates one birth every 8 seconds and one death every 11 seconds. Move the slider to change how quickly simulated time passes.

data and sources

  • Birth and death intervals: the 8-second birth interval and 11-second death interval match the U.S. Census Bureau’s projections for January 2020, published December 31, 2019. These are historical national averages, not current rates or observed individual events.
  • Starting population and date: 328,731,186 and April 8, 2020 are retained project settings; see the preserved project image. The original statistical source and reference date for that exact population have not been verified. April 8 is the simulation timestamp, not a confirmed population estimate date.
  • Map geography: state outlines use US Atlas 1.0.2 (2015 Census boundaries); county names and locations use US Atlas 3.0.1 (2017 Census boundaries).

The counter adds simulated births and subtracts simulated deaths from the starting population; it excludes migration. Counties are selected uniformly from the map’s available county locations, without population weighting. Dots and table rows are generated illustrations, not historical or live records.

For current official population projections, visit the U.S. Census Bureau Population Clock.
